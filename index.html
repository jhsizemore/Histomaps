History,
seen in streams.

A histomap is a visual argument about history. Civilizations, peoples, ideas and other historical subjects flow through time as streams — growing, shrinking, splitting, merging and disappearing while lives and events unfold within them.

The first map

Begin where the Histomap began.

John B. Sparks · 1931

Four thousand years on one continuous field.

In 1931, John B. Sparks published an extraordinary five-foot timeline whose flowing bands tried to show the relative power of contemporary states, nations and empires.

Histomaps.org begins with Sparks's idea, then asks what the form can become when it is interactive, extensible and explicit about the arguments built into its geometry.

The World Histomap adds events, people, lifelines and semantic zoom while preserving the defining feature of the form: history flows.

A historical visual tradition

The urge to see the world all at once is older than the web.

Historical maps, atlases and diagrams have always compressed complexity into a field you can scan. Histomaps add time to that field and make continuity itself visible.

The format

A Histomap makes its argument in geometry.

Time is continuous.

See what existed at the same moment rather than encountering history as isolated chapters.

Subjects are streams.

A civization, population, movement, species or idea can persist, transform, divide, merge and end.

Width means something.

Every Histomap should say what stream width represents. The answer can change from map to map.

People have scale.

Lifelines place individual lives inside the sweep of centuries and the structures around them.

The map is an argument.

Categories and measures are choices. A Histomap should expose them rather than pretending to be history itself.

Cartography as argument

Every map chooses what deserves space.

That choice becomes more explicit in a Histomap because the size and persistence of each stream are part of the claim being made.

Manifesto · August 2026

A Histomap Is an Argument About History

Most timelines flatten history into a sequence of dates. A histomap starts from continuity. Instead of asking only when did this happen?, it asks what was flowing through time when it happened?
