About the format

What is a Histomap?

A Histomap is a way of making historical continuity visible. Instead of reducing history to a row of dated events, it gives enduring subjects a shape through time. Streams can represent civilizations, populations, political movements, ideas, technologies or almost anything else with a historical life.

The width and structure of a stream are not neutral facts. They express the argument of the map. Every Histomap should therefore state what its widths mean, how its categories were chosen and where uncertainty enters the picture.

The historical origin

Why begin with John B. Sparks?

John B. Sparks's 1931 The Histomap: Four Thousand Years of World History is a remarkable early demonstration of the form. It compressed four thousand years of world history into a tall field of coloured streams representing Sparks's interpretation of the relative power of states, nations and empires.

Histomaps.org starts by rebuilding and interrogating that visual language rather than discarding it. The original also carries the assumptions of its era. Histomaps.org does not treat Sparks's judgments as neutral or final. His work is credited as the historical origin of this implementation and examined as an argument in its own right.

The David Rumsey Map Collection catalogues the 1931 original as a 31 × 158 cm timeline published by Histomap, Inc. and printed/distributed by Rand McNally.

The first publication

The World Histomap

The interactive World Histomap adds semantic zoom, events, historical people and lifelines, contextual cards and a developing stream model. It is a public preview and a research/design project in progress.

The point is not to turn Sparks's judgments into a definitive digital history. The point is to use the original map as the first test of a broader visual grammar and then improve that grammar through new maps, new measures and public scrutiny.

The larger project

Different maps. Different arguments.

The goal is a library of Histomaps: different subjects, different metrics, different arguments. Pacific History can centre Oceania rather than placing it at the edge. A United States Histomap can foreground institutional and political continuities that do not end cleanly at textbook chapter breaks. Domestication can give plants and animals streams of their own.

Eventually the format can move beyond a fixed-width field. Total scale itself could expand and contract. A Histomap of human population could widen over millennia, visibly contract during demographic catastrophes such as the Black Death, and then expand at extraordinary speed in the modern era. History would no longer have to behave as a zero-sum canvas.

Editorial method

The map should be contestable.

Every Histomap has to answer uncomfortable questions. Why is this stream here? Why is it this wide? Why did it change names? Why do two things merge? Why is one event shown and another missing?

Those are not problems to hide. They are the editorial work. As Histomaps.org develops, methodology, sources, confidence, disputed interpretations and alternate models should become increasingly visible. A Histomap should become more useful as a historical argument when you can see how it was made.

Current World Histomap: stream widths are interpretive historical power/influence, following and revising the logic of Sparks's original rather than claiming precise quantitative measurement.

Independent project

Help build the next Histomap.

Histomaps.org is intended to remain open to explore. A supporter programme is being prepared for the public launch, with development notes, early previews and opportunities to help choose which Histomaps are developed next.
